# Cold email to a prospective supervisor

Keep it under 250 words. Professors read these on their phones between meetings. The goal of the first email is not to
explain everything; it is to make them want to click one link.

**Before you send:** read two or three of the professor's recent papers properly, not just the abstracts. If you can't write the
"why you" sentence specifically and honestly, that professor is probably not the right fit. Send to one person at a time, never
a mass email with names swapped.

---

**Subject:** Prospective MSc/PhD student: fairness checks for small educational datasets

Dear Professor [Last name],

I'm a [final-year student / recent graduate] in [your programme] at Gannan Normal University, applying for [MSc/PhD] programs starting
[Fall 2027]. I read your paper "[title]" [one specific, honest sentence about what you took from it or a question it raised for
you, e.g. "and your point about X made me rethink how I evaluated Y"].

My undergraduate thesis asked when fairness mitigation in student-success prediction can be measured at all. On a
university dropout dataset (n = 3,630), every mitigation method I tested significantly reduced the equalized-odds gap; on a
school dataset with 395 students, none could be shown to work, and the "perfectly fair" solutions flagged everyone or no one.
From this I built a pre-flight check that tells practitioners, before they mitigate, whether their data can support a fairness
claim.

I have released the work as an open-source system with a decision-support dashboard:
- Code and paper summary: https://github.com/SadrulCrafts/fairpath
- 2-minute demo: <your-video-link>

I would be very interested in [specific direction from their work that connects, e.g. "fairness under distribution shift"
or "how advisors use explanations"]. Are you planning to take new students in [year]? If so, I'd be grateful for the chance to
talk briefly.

Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
MD Sadrul Anam
[email] · [LinkedIn] · [CV link]

---

## Notes

* **Don't overclaim.** Say "undergraduate thesis" and "open-source system". Don't call it a publication unless it is one.
* **Attach nothing** in the first email; link instead. Attachments from unknown senders often get filtered.
* **Follow up once**, after 10–14 days, with one line ("I wanted to follow up on my email below…"). If there is no reply after
  that, move on. Silence is normal and not personal.
* **Check the lab page first.** Many professors say on their website how they want to be contacted, or that they only admit
  through the department. Follow those instructions; it shows you read them.
* **Canadian specifics.** Many programs (e.g. at UBC, Toronto, Waterloo, McGill, Alberta, SFU) admit through a department
  application where you list potential supervisors. A short positive exchange with a professor beforehand still helps.
  Check each program's own deadlines and funding pages; they differ a lot.
